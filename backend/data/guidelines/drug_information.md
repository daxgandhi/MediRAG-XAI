# Drug Information Reference Sheet
## Metformin, Lisinopril, Aspirin, Atorvastatin, Omeprazole, Insulin, Amoxicillin
## Source: BNF / FDA Drug Label / WHO Model Formulary 2023

---

## METFORMIN (Glucophage)

**Class:** Biguanide antidiabetic
**Mechanism:** Reduces hepatic glucose production; improves insulin sensitivity; reduces intestinal glucose absorption.
**Indications:** Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (first-line), Prediabetes, Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)
**Dose:** 500–2000 mg/day in divided doses with meals. Max 2550 mg/day.
**Contraindications:** eGFR < 30 mL/min, hepatic failure, metabolic acidosis, radiologic contrast media (hold 48h), alcoholism
**Side Effects:** Nausea, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort (usually transient), lactic acidosis (rare), vitamin B12 deficiency
**Pregnancy Category:** B — Generally safe, preferred oral agent in gestational diabetes
**Key Monitoring:** eGFR (renal function), HbA1c, B12 levels annually
**Patient Tip:** Take with food to reduce GI side effects. Never crush extended-release tablets.

## LISINOPRIL (Zestril, Prinivil)

**Class:** ACE Inhibitor (Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme Inhibitor)
**Mechanism:** Inhibits ACE → reduces angiotensin II → vasodilation + reduced aldosterone
**Indications:** Hypertension, Heart failure (reduced EF), Diabetic nephropathy, Post-MI
**Dose:** 5–40 mg once daily
**Contraindications:** Pregnancy (Category D/X — ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED), Bilateral renal artery stenosis, Angioedema history, Hyperkalemia
**Side Effects:** Dry cough (10–15%), hyperkalemia, hypotension (first dose), angioedema (rare but serious)
**Drug Interactions:** NSAIDs (↓ efficacy + ↑ renal risk), K+-sparing diuretics (↑ hyperkalemia risk), lithium (↑ lithium toxicity)
**Key Monitoring:** Blood pressure, serum creatinine, serum potassium, eGFR
**Patient Tip:** If persistent dry cough develops, an ARB (like losartan) is a safe alternative.

## ASPIRIN (Acetylsalicylic Acid)

**Class:** NSAID / Antiplatelet
**Mechanism:** Irreversibly inhibits COX-1 and COX-2 → reduces prostaglandins, thromboxane A2
**Indications (Low Dose 75–100 mg):** Secondary prevention of MI and stroke, atrial fibrillation, ACS
**Indications (Full Dose 325–1000 mg):** Pain, fever, rheumatic conditions
**Contraindications:** Active GI bleeding/peptic ulcer, hemophilia, aspirin-exacerbated asthma, children < 16 (Reye syndrome risk), third trimester pregnancy
**Side Effects:** GI irritation, ulcers, bleeding (especially GI), tinnitus (salicylism with high doses)
**Key Interactions:** Warfarin (↑ bleeding), clopidogrel (↑ bleeding, avoid without indication), NSAIDs (↑ GI risk)
**Pregnancy:** AVOID in third trimester (risk of premature ductus arteriosus closure, maternal/neonatal bleeding)
**Patient Tip:** Take with food or milk to reduce gastric irritation.

## ATORVASTATIN (Lipitor)

**Class:** HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitor (Statin)
**Mechanism:** Inhibits HMG-CoA reductase → reduces hepatic cholesterol synthesis → upregulates LDL receptors
**Indications:** Hypercholesterolemia, mixed dyslipidemia, cardiovascular disease prevention (primary and secondary)
**Dose:** 10–80 mg once daily (at night)
**Contraindications:** Active liver disease, unexplained elevated transaminases, pregnancy (Category X), lactation
**Side Effects:** Myalgia (5–10%), myopathy, rhabdomyolysis (rare), elevated liver enzymes, headache
**Key Interactions:** Gemfibrozil (↑ myopathy risk), cyclosporine, clarithromycin, some antifungals (↑ statin levels)
**Key Monitoring:** Liver function tests (baseline, then if symptomatic), CK if muscle pain, lipid panel
**Patient Tip:** MUST STOP before pregnancy. Report muscle pain or weakness immediately.

## OMEPRAZOLE (Losec, Prilosec)

**Class:** Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI)
**Mechanism:** Irreversibly inhibits H+/K+-ATPase (proton pump) → reduces gastric acid secretion
**Indications:** GERD, peptic ulcer disease, H. pylori eradication (with antibiotics), NSAID-induced ulcer prevention, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
**Dose:** 20–40 mg once daily, 30–60 min before morning meal
**Side Effects:** Headache, nausea, diarrhea, hypomagnesemia (long-term), Clostridium difficile infection risk, fracture risk (long-term)
**Key Interactions:** Clopidogrel (PPI reduces antiplatelet effect — use pantoprazole instead), rilpivirine (↓ absorption)
**Long-term Use Risks:** Vitamin B12 deficiency, hypomagnesemia, bone fractures, kidney disease (rare)
**Patient Tip:** PPIs should not be used long-term without medical review.

## INSULIN (Various formulations)

**Class:** Hormone / Antidiabetic
**Types:**
- Rapid-acting: Insulin lispro, aspart (onset 15 min)
- Short-acting: Regular insulin (onset 30 min)
- Intermediate: NPH insulin (onset 2–4h)
- Long-acting: Glargine, detemir (once daily, no peak)
- Pre-mixed: 70/30, 50/50

**Indications:** Type 1 DM (all), Type 2 DM (inadequate control with orals), DKA, HHS, gestational DM
**Side Effects:** Hypoglycemia (most common), weight gain, lipodystrophy at injection sites, hypokalemia (during DKA treatment)
**Signs of Hypoglycemia:** Sweating, trembling, palpitations, confusion, hunger — treat with 15g fast-acting carbs (glucose tablets, juice, sugar)
**Storage:** Unopened: refrigerate (2–8°C). In use: room temp up to 28 days.
**Patient Tip:** Rotate injection sites. Never reuse needles. Monitor blood glucose before and after meals.

## AMOXICILLIN (Amoxil)

**Class:** Penicillin antibiotic (aminopenicillin)
**Mechanism:** Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins
**Indications:** Streptococcal pharyngitis, acute otitis media, sinusitis, community-acquired pneumonia (mild), UTI, Lyme disease, H. pylori (with omeprazole + clarithromycin)
**Dose:** 250–500 mg TDS or 875 mg BD; Children: 25–45 mg/kg/day divided
**Contraindications:** Penicillin allergy (risk of anaphylaxis)
**Side Effects:** Diarrhea (common), nausea, rash (especially with mononucleosis), anaphylaxis (rare but serious)
**Key Interactions:** Oral contraceptives (↓ efficacy — use backup contraception), warfarin (↑ bleeding risk), methotrexate
**Pregnancy Category:** B — Safe in pregnancy; preferred antibiotic for many infections
**Patient Tip:** Complete the full course even if feeling better to prevent antibiotic resistance.

# Heart Disease — Clinical Guidelines
## Source: ESC/AHA/ACC 2023 Guidelines

### Definition
Coronary Heart Disease (CHD), also called Ischemic Heart Disease (IHD), occurs when coronary arteries supplying the heart muscle become narrowed or blocked, usually due to atherosclerosis. This can lead to angina, myocardial infarction (heart attack), heart failure, or sudden cardiac death.

### Symptoms
- **Chest Pain (Angina):** Pressure, tightness, or squeezing sensation in chest, often radiating to left arm, jaw, or back
- **Shortness of breath:** On exertion or at rest (heart failure)
- **Palpitations:** Irregular or rapid heartbeat
- **Fatigue and weakness**
- **Edema:** Swelling of legs/ankles (heart failure)
- **Syncope (fainting)**
- **Silent MI:** Common in diabetics and elderly — no chest pain

### Risk Factors (ASCVD Risk)
- Hypertension
- Diabetes mellitus
- Dyslipidemia (high LDL, low HDL)
- Smoking
- Family history of premature CHD (men < 55, women < 65)
- Obesity / sedentary lifestyle
- Age
- Chronic kidney disease
- Inflammatory conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, lupus)

### Types of ACS (Acute Coronary Syndromes)
1. **Unstable Angina (UA):** No troponin rise; ischemia without infarction
2. **NSTEMI:** Non-ST elevation MI; partial occlusion; troponin elevated
3. **STEMI:** ST elevation MI; complete coronary occlusion; emergency reperfusion needed

### Diagnosis
- **ECG:** ST elevation/depression, T-wave changes, new LBBB
- **Cardiac Biomarkers:** Troponin I/T (most sensitive/specific), CK-MB, Myoglobin
- **Echocardiography:** Wall motion abnormalities, EF assessment
- **Coronary Angiography (Gold Standard):** Defines anatomy for PCI/CABG decision
- **Stress Testing (Exercise ECG, Nuclear, Echo Stress):** For stable angina workup

### Acute MI Management (STEMI Protocol)
1. **MONA** (Morphine, Oxygen, Nitrates, Aspirin) — first aid
2. **Primary PCI** (preferred if available within 90–120 min of first contact)
3. **Thrombolysis** (if PCI not available within 120 min) — Alteplase, Streptokinase
4. Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT): Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor (Ticagrelor/Clopidogrel)
5. Anticoagulation: Heparin (UFH or LMWH)
6. Beta-blockers (within 24h if hemodynamically stable)
7. ACE Inhibitor/ARB: within 24h
8. Statin: High-intensity (Atorvastatin 80 mg) within 24h

### Secondary Prevention (Post-MI)
- Aspirin 75 mg lifelong
- P2Y12 inhibitor (at least 12 months)
- Statin (LDL target < 70 mg/dL)
- Beta-blocker (at least 1 year)
- ACE inhibitor (especially if EF < 40% or diabetes)
- Cardiac rehabilitation program

### Heart Failure Management
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs/ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan)
- Beta-blockers (Carvedilol, Bisoprolol, Metoprolol)
- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (Spironolactone)
- SGLT-2 inhibitors (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin) — reduced hospitalizations
- Diuretics (Furosemide) — for congestion

### Lifestyle Modifications
- Mediterranean diet (reduces CV events by 30%)
- Exercise: 150 min/week moderate-intensity aerobic activity
- Smoking cessation (most important modifiable risk factor)
- Alcohol: < 2 units/day
- Weight management (BMI < 25)
- BP target: < 130/80 mmHg
- LDL target: < 70 mg/dL for very high risk

### References
- ESC Guidelines for Acute Coronary Syndromes 2023
- ACC/AHA Guideline on Primary Prevention of CVD 2019
- ESC Guidelines on Heart Failure 2023

# Malaria and Dengue — WHO Clinical Guidelines

## MALARIA (WHO 2023)

### Causative Agent
*Plasmodium* species: *P. falciparum* (most severe), *P. vivax*, *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, *P. knowlesi*
Vector: Female *Anopheles* mosquito

### Symptoms
- Fever (typically cyclical: every 48–72 hours)
- Chills and rigors
- Headache
- Muscle and joint pain
- Nausea, vomiting
- Fatigue and weakness
- Splenomegaly (chronic infection)
- **Severe malaria (*P. falciparum*):** Cerebral malaria (seizures, coma), respiratory distress, renal failure, severe anemia, hypoglycemia, pulmonary edema

### Diagnosis
- **Thick and Thin Blood Smear:** Gold standard; identifies species
- **Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs):** Detects malaria antigens; results in 15 min
- **PCR:** Most sensitive; for low-density parasitemia

### WHO Treatment Protocol
**Uncomplicated *P. falciparum*:**
- **Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT)** — First-line worldwide
  - Artemether-lumefantrine (AL) — 3 days
  - Artesunate-amodiaquine
  - Artesunate-mefloquine
  - Dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine

**Severe Malaria:**
- IV/IM Artesunate (preferred) — switch to ACT when able to tolerate oral
- IV Quinine (alternative if artesunate not available)

**P. vivax/ovale:**
- Chloroquine (where sensitive) + Primaquine (14 days for radical cure)
- Screen for G6PD deficiency before primaquine (risk of hemolysis)

### Prevention
- Long-lasting insecticidal nets (LLINs)
- Indoor residual spraying (IRS)
- Chemoprophylaxis for travelers: Atovaquone-proguanil, Mefloquine, Doxycycline

## DENGUE (WHO 2023)

### Causative Agent
Dengue virus (DENV 1–4 serotypes) — *Aedes aegypti* mosquito vector

### Symptoms (3 Phases)
**Febrile Phase (Days 1–3):** Sudden high fever (40°C), severe headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia, arthralgia ("breakbone fever"), nausea, vomiting, facial flushing

**Critical Phase (Days 4–6):** Plasma leakage — pleural effusion, ascites; risk of Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever (DHF) or Dengue Shock Syndrome (DSS); thrombocytopenia, elevated hematocrit

**Recovery Phase (Days 7–10):** Reabsorption of leaked fluid, rash, bradycardia

### Warning Signs (WHO)
- Abdominal pain or tenderness
- Persistent vomiting
- Clinical fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion)
- Mucosal bleeding
- Lethargy or restlessness
- Liver enlargement > 2 cm
- Rapidly rising hematocrit with rapid decline in platelets

### Diagnosis
- NS1 antigen (Days 1–5): Most sensitive early
- IgM/IgG serology (Day 5+): ELISA-based
- Dengue PCR (Days 1–5): Most specific
- CBC: Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, elevated hematocrit

### Treatment
**No specific antiviral treatment.**
- Supportive care: IV fluids (careful titration), paracetamol for fever
- **AVOID ASPIRIN AND NSAIDs** (↑ bleeding risk)
- Platelet transfusion only if < 20,000/µL OR active bleeding
- Monitor: CBC, hematocrit, urine output daily during critical phase

### References
- WHO Guidelines for Treatment of Malaria, 3rd Edition 2015 (updated 2023)
- WHO Dengue: Guidelines for Diagnosis, Treatment, Prevention and Control 2009 (updated protocols 2023)

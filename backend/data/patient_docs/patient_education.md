## What is Diabetes?
Diabetes is a disease where your body can't properly control the amount of sugar (glucose) in your blood. Glucose comes from the food you eat and is your body's main source of energy. Normally, insulin (a hormone made by the pancreas) helps glucose enter your cells. In diabetes, either your body doesn't make enough insulin, or it doesn't use insulin well, causing glucose to build up in your blood.

**There are two main types:**
- **Type 1 Diabetes:** Your immune system destroys the cells that make insulin. You need to take insulin every day.
- **Type 2 Diabetes:** Your body doesn't use insulin well. This is the most common type and is often linked to lifestyle factors.

**Key warning signs:** Frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing sores.

**What you can do:**
- Monitor your blood sugar regularly
- Take your medicines as prescribed
- Eat a healthy diet (less sugar, more fiber)
- Exercise regularly (30 min walks, 5 days/week)
- See your doctor regularly

## What are the Side Effects of Metformin?
Metformin is a common medicine for Type 2 Diabetes. It helps lower blood sugar by reducing the amount of glucose your liver makes.

**Common side effects (especially when starting):**
- Nausea, vomiting, stomach upset
- Diarrhea or loose stools
- Loss of appetite
- Metallic taste in mouth
These usually improve after the first few weeks. Taking metformin with food helps.

**Serious but rare side effects:**
- **Lactic Acidosis:** Very rare but serious buildup of lactic acid. Signs: unusual muscle pain, weakness, trouble breathing, stomach pain. Seek emergency care.
- **Vitamin B12 deficiency:** Long-term use can lower B12 levels. Your doctor may check this.

**What to do:**
- Always take metformin with or after food
- Do not stop suddenly without talking to your doctor
- Tell your doctor about all other medicines you take
- Avoid excessive alcohol

## How Can I Manage Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)?
High blood pressure (hypertension) means the force of blood pushing against your artery walls is too high. It's often called the "silent killer" because it has no symptoms but can cause heart attacks and strokes.

**Lifestyle changes that help:**
1. **Reduce salt (sodium):** Aim for less than 2,300 mg of sodium per day. Avoid processed foods, canned soups, and fast food.
2. **DASH Diet:** Eat more fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy. Less saturated fat and cholesterol.
3. **Exercise regularly:** At least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week (brisk walking, swimming, cycling).
4. **Lose weight:** Even losing 5–10 pounds can lower blood pressure.
5. **Limit alcohol:** No more than 1 drink/day for women, 2 for men.
6. **Quit smoking:** Smoking raises blood pressure and damages blood vessels.
7. **Reduce stress:** Practice deep breathing, yoga, meditation.
8. **Monitor BP at home:** Keep a log and bring it to doctor visits.

**Medications:**
- Your doctor may prescribe medicines like lisinopril, amlodipine, or hydrochlorothiazide.
- Take them every day — don't stop because you feel fine.
- High blood pressure rarely causes symptoms until it's very high.

## What is Asthma?
Asthma is a chronic disease where the airways in your lungs become inflamed and narrowed, making it hard to breathe. Triggers include allergens (dust mites, pollen, pet dander), cold air, exercise, smoke, and respiratory infections.

**Symptoms:**
- Wheezing (whistling sound when breathing)
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Coughing, especially at night or early morning

**Management:**
- **Reliever inhaler (Blue):** Salbutamol (albuterol) — use for quick relief of symptoms. Use before exercise to prevent exercise-induced asthma.
- **Preventer inhaler (Brown/Orange):** Corticosteroid inhaler (beclometasone, fluticasone) — use EVERY DAY even when you feel well. This reduces inflammation.
- **Avoid triggers:** Dust, smoke, strong odors, cold air.
- **Asthma action plan:** Know what to do if symptoms get worse.

**Asthma Attack Emergency Signs:**
- Severe shortness of breath
- Reliever inhaler not working
- Can't speak in full sentences
- Lips or fingernails turning blue
→ CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES IMMEDIATELY (112 / 911)

## What is Heart Disease?
Heart disease refers to conditions that affect how your heart works. The most common type is coronary artery disease (CAD), where the arteries supplying your heart get narrowed by fatty deposits (plaque).

**Warning signs of a heart attack:**
- Chest pain or pressure (may spread to arm, jaw, back)
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness
- **In women:** Fatigue, jaw pain, upper back pain (may not have classic chest pain)

If you think you or someone else is having a heart attack: **CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY. Do not drive yourself.** Chew an aspirin (325 mg) if not allergic.

**Prevention:**
- Don't smoke
- Control blood pressure and cholesterol
- Exercise regularly
- Eat a heart-healthy diet
- Manage stress
- Control diabetes

## Questions About COVID-19
COVID-19 is caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus. It spreads mainly through respiratory droplets.

**Symptoms:** Fever, cough, fatigue, shortness of breath, loss of taste/smell, body aches, headache. Some infections are mild or asymptomatic.

**Severe disease warning signs:**
- Difficulty breathing
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- Confusion or inability to stay awake
- Bluish lips or face
→ Seek emergency care immediately

**Prevention:**
- Vaccination (highly effective against severe disease)
- Wear masks in crowded/indoor settings
- Good hand hygiene (soap and water for 20 sec, or 60% alcohol sanitizer)
- Stay home if sick

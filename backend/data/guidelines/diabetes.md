# Diabetes Mellitus — Clinical Guidelines
## Source: WHO / IDF / ADA 2023 Recommendations

### Definition
Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose levels (hyperglycemia) resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both. Type 1 DM involves absolute insulin deficiency; Type 2 DM involves insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency.

### Diagnostic Criteria (WHO/ADA)
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two occasions
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL during 75g OGTT
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms

Prediabetes: Fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL; HbA1c 5.7–6.4%

### Symptoms
- Polyuria (frequent urination)
- Polydipsia (excessive thirst)
- Polyphagia (excessive hunger)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue and weakness
- Blurred vision
- Slow wound healing
- Recurrent infections
- Tingling/numbness in hands or feet (neuropathy)

### Classification
1. **Type 1 DM** — Autoimmune destruction of beta cells; requires insulin
2. **Type 2 DM** — Most common; insulin resistance + relative deficiency
3. **Gestational DM** — During pregnancy; increases risk of Type 2 later
4. **MODY** — Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young (genetic)

### Management Goals
- HbA1c < 7.0% for most patients (individualize)
- Fasting glucose: 80–130 mg/dL
- Postprandial glucose: < 180 mg/dL
- Blood pressure: < 130/80 mmHg
- LDL < 100 mg/dL (< 70 mg/dL if high CV risk)

### First-Line Treatment
- **Metformin** — First-line for Type 2 DM; contraindicated with eGFR < 30
- Lifestyle modification: diet, exercise (150 min/week moderate activity)
- Weight management (5–10% weight loss improves glycemic control)

### Second-Line Agents
- GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide) — if CV disease or obesity
- SGLT-2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) — if heart failure or CKD
- DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin) — well tolerated, weight neutral
- Sulfonylureas — low cost, risk of hypoglycemia
- Insulin — for Type 1 and advanced Type 2

### Monitoring
- Self-monitoring blood glucose (SMBG): before meals and at bedtime
- HbA1c: every 3 months until stable, then every 6 months
- Annual: kidney function, lipid panel, urine albumin, eye exam, foot exam, ECG

### Complications
**Acute:** Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA), Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS), Hypoglycemia
**Chronic Microvascular:** Retinopathy, Nephropathy, Neuropathy
**Chronic Macrovascular:** CAD, Stroke, Peripheral Arterial Disease

### Patient Education Points
1. Monitor blood sugar daily
2. Take medications as prescribed — do not skip insulin
3. Follow a low-carbohydrate, high-fiber diet
4. Exercise regularly (walking 30 min daily)
5. Check feet daily for cuts, sores, or swelling
6. Never miss follow-up appointments
7. Recognize hypoglycemia: sweating, trembling, confusion — treat with 15g fast-acting carbs

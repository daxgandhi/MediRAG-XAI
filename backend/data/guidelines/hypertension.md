# Hypertension — Clinical Guidelines
## Source: WHO / JNC 8 / ESC/ESH 2023

### Definition
Hypertension (high blood pressure) is defined as persistent elevation of systolic BP ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic BP ≥ 90 mmHg. It is a major risk factor for cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, and renal disease.

### Classification (ESC/ESH 2023)
| Category | Systolic (mmHg) | Diastolic (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|
| Optimal | < 120 | < 80 |
| Normal | 120–129 | 80–84 |
| High Normal | 130–139 | 85–89 |
| Grade 1 HT | 140–159 | 90–99 |
| Grade 2 HT | 160–179 | 100–109 |
| Grade 3 HT | ≥ 180 | ≥ 110 |
| Isolated Systolic HT | ≥ 140 | < 90 |

### Symptoms
Most patients are asymptomatic ("silent killer"). Symptoms may include:
- Severe headache (occipital)
- Visual disturbances
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Epistaxis (nosebleed)
- Nausea and vomiting (hypertensive crisis)

### Causes
- **Primary (Essential):** 90–95%; no identifiable cause; genetic + lifestyle
- **Secondary:** Renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, thyroid disorders, obstructive sleep apnea, medications (OCPs, NSAIDs, steroids)

### Risk Factors
- Age (men > 55, women > 65)
- Family history
- Obesity (BMI > 30)
- Physical inactivity
- High sodium diet
- Excessive alcohol
- Smoking
- Diabetes mellitus
- Dyslipidemia
- Chronic stress

### Treatment Goals
- General patients: < 130/80 mmHg
- Elderly (> 65 years): < 140/90 mmHg (systolic 130–139)
- Diabetes or CKD: < 130/80 mmHg
- High CV risk: < 130/80 mmHg

### Non-Pharmacological Treatment (Lifestyle)
- DASH diet (low sodium, high potassium, low saturated fat)
- Sodium restriction: < 2.3 g/day (ideally < 1.5 g/day)
- Weight loss: reduce BP by 1 mmHg per kg lost
- Physical activity: 150 min/week moderate aerobic exercise
- Alcohol reduction: < 2 drinks/day men, < 1 drink/day women
- Smoking cessation (reduces total CV risk)
- Stress reduction (meditation, yoga)

### First-Line Drug Therapy
Four major classes recommended:
1. **ACE Inhibitors** (Lisinopril, Ramipril) — especially with diabetes, CKD, heart failure
2. **ARBs** (Losartan, Valsartan) — if ACE inhibitor cough intolerable
3. **Calcium Channel Blockers** (Amlodipine) — well tolerated, good for elderly
4. **Thiazide Diuretics** (Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone) — good first-line, low cost

**Note:** ACE Inhibitors and ARBs are CONTRAINDICATED in pregnancy (Category D/X).

### Drug Combinations
- For Grade 2–3 HT: Start with two drugs
- Preferred: ACEi/ARB + CCB or ACEi/ARB + Thiazide
- Avoid combining ACEi + ARB (dual RAAS blockade increases adverse effects)

### Monitoring
- BP measurements: at home twice daily (morning + evening) for 1 week
- Clinic BP check: every 1–3 months until controlled, then 6-monthly
- Labs: Serum creatinine, potassium, glucose, lipids — annually
- ECG: baseline and annually
- Ophthalmology: annual fundoscopy for grade 2+ HT

### Hypertensive Urgency vs Emergency
- **Urgency:** BP ≥ 180/120 mmHg without end-organ damage — oral therapy, reduce over 24–48 hours
- **Emergency:** BP ≥ 180/120 mmHg WITH end-organ damage (encephalopathy, ACS, aortic dissection) — IV therapy in ICU

### Complications
- Stroke (most common cause of stroke worldwide)
- Myocardial infarction
- Heart failure
- Chronic kidney disease
- Aortic aneurysm
- Peripheral arterial disease
- Hypertensive retinopathy

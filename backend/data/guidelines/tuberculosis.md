# Tuberculosis (TB) — WHO Clinical Guidelines
## Source: WHO Global TB Report 2023 / WHO Consolidated Guidelines on TB Treatment

### Definition
Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. It primarily affects the lungs (pulmonary TB) but can affect any organ (extrapulmonary TB). TB is the world's leading cause of death from a single infectious agent.

### Transmission
- Airborne: droplet nuclei (1–5 µm) from coughing, sneezing, or speaking
- Close contact is required for transmission
- Only active pulmonary TB is infectious; latent TB is not

### Types
1. **Latent TB Infection (LTBI)** — Infected, no symptoms, not infectious
2. **Active Pulmonary TB** — Symptomatic, infectious, culture-positive
3. **Extrapulmonary TB** — Lymph nodes, pleura, CNS (TB meningitis), bone, kidney, abdomen

### Symptoms (Active Pulmonary TB)
- Persistent cough > 2–3 weeks (may be productive or dry)
- Hemoptysis (coughing blood)
- Night sweats
- Low-grade fever (afternoon)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue and loss of appetite
- Chest pain and dyspnea (advanced disease)

### Diagnosis
- **Sputum Smear Microscopy:** Acid-fast bacilli (AFB); sensitivity 45–80%
- **GeneXpert MTB/RIF (Xpert):** Rapid PCR test; WHO-recommended first test
- **Sputum Culture (Gold Standard):** Lowenstein-Jensen medium; 6–8 weeks; also gives drug sensitivity
- **CXR:** Apical/upper lobe infiltrates, cavities, lymphadenopathy
- **Tuberculin Skin Test (TST/Mantoux):** ≥ 10 mm induration indicates infection (not disease)
- **IGRA (QuantiFERON-TB):** Better for LTBI diagnosis; not affected by BCG vaccine

### WHO-Recommended Standard TB Treatment (DS-TB)
**Intensive Phase (2 months):** HRZE
- H = Isoniazid (INH)
- R = Rifampicin
- Z = Pyrazinamide
- E = Ethambutol

**Continuation Phase (4 months):** HR
- H = Isoniazid
- R = Rifampicin

Total duration: **6 months** for most drug-susceptible TB.

### Drug-Resistant TB Treatment
- **MDR-TB** (resistant to INH + Rifampicin): Bedaquiline-based regimens (18–24 months)
- **XDR-TB** (resistant to MDR + fluoroquinolones + injectable): Bedaquiline + linezolid + clofazimine

### Monitoring During Treatment
- Sputum smear: months 2, 5, 6 (end)
- Liver function tests (LFTs) — monthly if risk factors (hepatitis, alcoholism)
- Visual acuity — for ethambutol
- Adherence: DOTS (Directly Observed Therapy, Short-course) is WHO standard

### Side Effects of TB Drugs
- **INH:** Peripheral neuropathy (supplement B6/pyridoxine), hepatotoxicity
- **Rifampicin:** Orange urine/secretions, hepatotoxicity, reduces OCP efficacy
- **Pyrazinamide:** Hyperuricemia/gout, hepatotoxicity
- **Ethambutol:** Optic neuritis (dose-dependent), monitor visual acuity

### Infection Control
- Airborne isolation (negative pressure room) for hospitalized active TB
- N95 respirator for healthcare workers
- BCG vaccination (neonates) — protects against severe childhood TB
- LTBI treatment to prevent progression: INH 6 months or 3HP (INH + Rifapentine weekly × 12 doses)

### Special Populations
- **HIV + TB:** Initiate ART within 8 weeks of TB treatment start; use Rifabutin if on NNRTI/PI
- **Pregnancy:** INH + Rifampicin + Ethambutol (avoid Pyrazinamide in 1st trimester); supplement B6
- **Children:** Weight-based dosing; preferred formulations: FDC tablets

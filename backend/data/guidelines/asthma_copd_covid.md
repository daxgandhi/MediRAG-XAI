# BRONCHIAL ASTHMA — GINA 2023

### Definition
Asthma is a heterogeneous disease characterized by chronic airway inflammation and variable airflow limitation. It is defined by a history of respiratory symptoms such as wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness and cough that vary over time and in intensity, together with variable expiratory airflow limitation.

### Symptoms
- **Wheeze** — high-pitched whistling sound when breathing out
- **Shortness of breath** — especially with exertion or at night
- **Chest tightness or pain**
- **Cough** — especially at night or early morning, may be dry or productive
- Symptoms that **worsen at night or early morning**
- Symptoms triggered by: exercise, cold air, smoke, allergens, respiratory infections

### Diagnosis
- Spirometry: FEV1/FVC < 0.75–0.80 (obstruction)
- Bronchodilator reversibility: FEV1 increase ≥ 12% and 200 mL after salbutamol
- Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF): variability ≥ 10% supports diagnosis
- Positive methacholine/exercise challenge

### GINA Treatment Steps (2023)
| Step | Preferred Controller | Reliever |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | None (low-dose ICS-formoterol PRN) | ICS-formoterol |
| Step 2 | Low-dose ICS daily | SABA PRN |
| Step 3 | Low-dose ICS-LABA | ICS-formoterol |
| Step 4 | Medium-dose ICS-LABA | ICS-formoterol |
| Step 5 | High-dose ICS-LABA + add-on therapy | ICS-formoterol |

**Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS):** Beclometasone, Budesonide, Fluticasone — cornerstone of treatment
**LABA:** Long-acting beta-2 agonists — Formoterol, Salmeterol (always with ICS, never alone)
**SABA:** Short-acting beta-2 agonist — Salbutamol (Albuterol) — quick reliever

### Asthma Exacerbation Management
**Mild-Moderate:**
- SABA every 20 min × 3, then hourly
- Oral prednisolone 40–50 mg/day × 5–7 days
- Monitor SpO2 (target > 92%)

**Severe/Life-threatening:**
- IV/nebulized salbutamol
- IV magnesium sulfate
- IV hydrocortisone
- Heliox if available
- NIV or mechanical ventilation if needed

### Triggers to Avoid
- Cigarette smoke (active and passive)
- Dust mites, animal dander, pollen
- Exercise (use pre-exercise bronchodilator)
- NSAIDs/aspirin in aspirin-sensitive asthma
- Beta-blockers (contraindicated in asthma)
- Strong odors, chemicals

# COPD — GOLD 2023

### Definition
COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease) is a heterogeneous lung condition characterized by chronic respiratory symptoms (dyspnea, cough, sputum production) due to abnormalities of the airways (bronchitis, bronchiolitis) and/or alveoli (emphysema) that cause persistent, progressive airflow obstruction.

### Symptoms
- Chronic progressive dyspnea (shortness of breath)
- Chronic cough (productive — "smoker's cough")
- Sputum production (white/clear, may become purulent during exacerbations)
- Wheezing
- Chest tightness
- Barrel chest, use of accessory muscles (advanced disease)

### Diagnosis
- **Spirometry (Gold Standard):** Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70
- **GOLD ABCD classification:**
  - Spirometry grade (GOLD 1–4): FEV1 % predicted (>80%, 50-79%, 30-49%, <30%)
  - Symptom burden: mMRC scale, CAT score
  - Exacerbation history

### Risk Factors
- Cigarette smoking (most important — 80% of cases)
- Indoor air pollution (biomass fuel combustion)
- Occupational exposures (dust, chemicals, fumes)
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (genetic)
- Childhood respiratory infections

### GOLD 2023 Treatment
**Bronchodilators (mainstay of COPD management):**
- Short-acting: SABA (salbutamol), SAMA (ipratropium)
- Long-acting: LABA (formoterol, salmeterol), LAMA (tiotropium, umeclidinium)

**Group A:** Short-acting bronchodilator PRN
**Group B:** Long-acting bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA)
**Group E (frequent exacerbations):** LABA + LAMA; add ICS if blood eosinophils ≥ 300 cells/µL

**Exacerbation management:**
- Systemic corticosteroids (prednisolone 40 mg × 5 days)
- Antibiotics if purulent sputum or severe exacerbation
- SABA + SAMA nebulized
- Controlled oxygen therapy (target SpO2 88–92%)
- NIV for hypercapnic respiratory failure

**Non-pharmacological:**
- **Smoking cessation** — most effective intervention
- Pulmonary rehabilitation
- Influenza and pneumococcal vaccination
- Oxygen therapy if PaO2 < 55 mmHg

# COVID-19 — WHO Clinical Management Guidelines 2023

### Causative Agent
SARS-CoV-2 virus (Betacoronavirus). Multiple variants including Alpha, Delta, Omicron (dominant since 2022).

### Transmission
- Primarily respiratory droplets and aerosols
- Fomite transmission possible but less common
- Pre-symptomatic and asymptomatic transmission documented

### Clinical Classification

| Severity | Definition |
|---|---|
| Mild | Symptoms without pneumonia or hypoxia (SpO2 ≥ 95%) |
| Moderate | Clinical pneumonia, SpO2 ≥ 90% |
| Severe | Severe pneumonia, SpO2 < 90%, RR > 30/min |
| Critical | ARDS, septic shock, multi-organ failure |

### Symptoms
**Common:** Fever, cough, fatigue, headache, myalgia, sore throat, rhinorrhea
**Specific:** Loss of taste/smell (anosmia/ageusia) — more common with original strains
**Severe:** Shortness of breath, persistent chest pain, confusion, cyanosis

### High-Risk Groups
- Age > 65 years
- Obesity (BMI > 30)
- Diabetes mellitus
- Cardiovascular disease
- Chronic lung disease (COPD, asthma)
- Chronic kidney/liver disease
- Immunocompromised patients
- Unvaccinated individuals

### Diagnosis
- **RT-PCR (Gold Standard):** Nasopharyngeal/oropharyngeal swab; most sensitive
- **Rapid Antigen Test (RAT):** Faster, less sensitive; suitable for high-prevalence settings
- **Chest CT:** Ground-glass opacities, peripheral bilateral distribution (not required for diagnosis)
- **CXR:** Bilateral infiltrates in severe disease

### WHO Recommended Treatment (2023)
**Mild (non-hospitalized):**
- Supportive care: hydration, antipyretics (paracetamol — NOT aspirin/NSAIDs for fever)
- **Nirmatrelvir-ritonavir (Paxlovid)** — within 5 days of symptoms for high-risk patients
- **Molnupiravir** — alternative oral antiviral

**Moderate (hospitalized, no O2):**
- Supportive care + monitoring
- **Baricitinib** (JAK inhibitor) — recommended
- Awake prone positioning

**Severe (O2 required):**
- **Dexamethasone 6 mg/day × 10 days** — reduces mortality (RECOVERY trial)
- **IL-6 inhibitors:** Tocilizumab or Sarilumab
- **Baricitinib**
- **Awake prone positioning**
- Anticoagulation (prophylactic LMWH) for all hospitalized patients
- **Remdesivir** — consider in early severe disease

**Critical (ICU):**
- Mechanical ventilation: lung-protective (low tidal volume 6 mL/kg)
- Prone ventilation ≥ 12 hours/day
- High-dose anticoagulation if thrombosis
- Vasopressors for septic shock

### Infection Prevention
- Vaccination: highly effective against severe disease and death
- High-quality masks (N95/FFP2) in indoor crowded settings
- Hand hygiene
- Ventilation/air circulation
- Isolation of confirmed/suspected cases

### Long COVID (Post-COVID Condition)
Defined as symptoms persisting ≥ 3 months after acute infection:
- Fatigue, cognitive impairment ("brain fog"), dyspnea, palpitations, joint pain
- Management: multidisciplinary, symptomatic, rehabilitation
